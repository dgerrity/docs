# Statement of Purpose
> Dan Gerrity, December, 2024


Education is the cornerstone of a just and equitable society. It is the singularly effective method to empower individuals to discover and develop their natural gifts in the face of structural and systemic barriers to create opportunities for themselves and their communities. Yet, significant equity gaps persist in the U.S. education system, as research highlights disparities in academic achievement, access to resources, funding, and availability of teachers based on socioeconomic and cultural factors. I believe the most effective way to close these gaps is by intervening early in students’ educational journeys. With a lifelong commitment to equity and social justice, I am ready to focus on direct impact, becoming a teacher, and using education as a tool to empower historically underserved populations.

Education has transformative power. It equips students with the knowledge, confidence, and critical thinking skills to challenge inequities and advocate for change. Growing up, I was deeply influenced by my mother, a pioneering feminist and the founder of the Women’s Resource Center at the College of Wooster. Through her work, I learned at an impressionable age about systemic inequities and the importance of advocating for marginalized groups. These lessons shaped my worldview and reinforced my belief in the necessity of inclusive and empowering education.

As a technology executive, I spent decades addressing diversity, equity, and inclusion challenges within the corporate world.  During those times, my most effective application of energy in the DE&I context was recruiting. My teams and I have hired over 1,000 individuals during that career, and I have personally conducted over 500 interviews.  I led initiatives to recruit, develop, and promote talent from underrepresented backgrounds, including creating a hiring program at Morgan State University, an HBCU, and growing that program to nearly two dozen HSIs and MSIs. My work at Morgan State taught me the importance of identifying and nurturing potential. I helped students translate their unique experiences into language that resonated with (white male) interviewers, enabling them to break into careers that transformed their socioeconomic trajectories. This experience reinforced my belief in the power of education to open doors and change lives.

While we grew this program and my team eventually grew my employer's African American population by a full percentage point, I realized that attempting to fill equity gaps just before students enter the job market is simply too late to address many of the important structural inequities faced. This insight inspired my decision to move from having systemic impact into direct impact: teaching, where I can make a direct impact earlier in students’ lives.

My passion for working with diverse populations extends beyond professional settings. I have studied Portuguese, German, and Spanish, striving for fluency to deepen my understanding of different cultures. These language skills not only broaden my global perspective but also inform my commitment to developing bilingual teaching abilities. I see language as an important gateway to building meaningful connections with students and their families, particularly in underserved communities.

The U-ACT program’s emphasis on social justice and serving diverse communities aligns with my values and aspirations. I am particularly drawn to its focus on preparing educators to meet the needs of historically underserved students. My vision for teaching is to create inclusive classrooms where all students feel valued and endowed with unique skills and aptitudes. I hope to students discover those aptitudes and recognize them so that each can reach their fullest potential. Given my career and academic training, I am especially enthusiastic about teaching STEM subjects, as they are critical for success in our increasingly technical world. I recognize the State of Washington has a duty to employ highly qualified teachers and I am prepared to take a role consistent with the needs of the schools and education department, whether STEM or something different.

At the University of Washington, I aim to develop the skills and knowledge to become a teacher who not only educates but also inspires. I bring to the U-ACT program a unique combination of professional expertise, a global perspective, and a deep commitment to equity. By learning from faculty and collaborating with peers, I hope to contribute meaningfully to the College of Education and more importantly to the lives of the students I will serve.
