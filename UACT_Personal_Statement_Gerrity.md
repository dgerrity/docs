# Statement of Purpose
> Dan Gerrity, December, 2024


Education is the cornerstone of a just and equitable society. It is the singularly effective method to empower individuals to discover and develop their natural gifts in the face of structural and systemic barriers so that they may create opportunities for themselves and their communities. Yet, significant equity gaps persist in the U.S. educational system, as research highlights growing disparities in academic achievement, access to resources, funding, and availability of teachers based on socioeconomic and cultural factors. The most effective way to close these gaps is to intervene early in students’ educational journeys, emphasizing discovering and accelerating potential. With a lifelong commitment to equity and social justice, I am ready to focus on directly impacting students, becoming a teacher, and using education to empower members of historically underserved populations.

Education has transformative power. It equips students with the knowledge, confidence, and critical thinking skills to challenge inequities and advocate for change. Growing up, I was deeply influenced by my mother, a pioneering feminist and the founder of the Women’s Resource Center at the College of Wooster. Through her work, I learned at an impressionable age about systemic inequities and the importance of advocating for marginalized groups. These lessons shaped my worldview and reinforced my belief in the necessity of inclusive and empowering education.

As a technology executive, I spent decades addressing diversity, equity, and inclusion challenges within the largely resistant corporate world. During that time, my most effective application of energy in the DE&I context was on recruiting, where I felt I could make an immediate and measurable difference. My teams and I hired over 1,000 individuals worldwide during that career, and I have personally conducted over 500 interviews. I led initiatives to recruit, develop, and promote talent from underrepresented backgrounds, including creating a hiring program at Morgan State University, an HBCU, and subsequently expanding that program to nearly two dozen HBCUs, HSIs, and MSIs. My work at Morgan State taught me the importance of identifying possibility -- despite institutional bias -- and then nurturing that possibility into realization. I helped students identify and translate their valid and unique experiences into language that resonated with (typically white male) interviewers, enabling the students to break into careers that transformed their socioeconomic trajectories and, more importantly, helped them see value in themselves. This experience reinforced my belief in the power of education to open doors and change lives.

While we grew this program and my team eventually grew my employer's African American population by a whole percentage point, I realized that attempting to fill equity gaps just before students enter the job market is too late to address many of the essential structural inequities faced - those inequities had already thwarted many worthy students. This insight inspired my decision to move from having a broad impact on thousands to having a direct impact on  dozens of students earlier in their lives.

My passion for working with diverse populations extends beyond professional settings. I have studied abroad in Austria and Brazil and have learned Portuguese, German, and most recently Spanish, striving for fluency to deepen my understanding of different cultures. These language skills broaden my global perspective and inform my commitment to developing bilingual teaching abilities. Language is an important gateway to building meaningful connections with students and their families, particularly in underserved communities.

The U-ACT program’s emphasis on social justice and serving diverse communities aligns with my values and aspirations. I am particularly drawn to its focus on preparing educators to meet the needs of historically underserved students. My vision for teaching is to create inclusive classrooms where each and every student feels valued and endowed with unique skills and aptitudes. I intend to help students discover and recognize aptitudes so that each can develop towards their fullest potential. Given my career and academic training, I am incredibly enthusiastic about teaching STEM subjects, as they are critical for success in our increasingly technical world, and I have career experience across a range of scientific disciplines. While I am excited about those subjects due to preparation, I fully recognize the State of Washington has a duty to employ highly qualified teachers, and I am prepared to take a role consistent with the needs of the schools and the department of education, whether in STEM, language, social studies or whatever might be in highest need.

At the University of Washington, I aim to develop the skills and knowledge to become a teacher who educates and inspires. I bring to the U-ACT program a unique combination of professional expertise, a global perspective, and a deep commitment to equity. By learning from faculty and collaborating with peers, I hope to contribute meaningfully to the College of Education and, more importantly, to the lives of the students I will serve.
